# Personality & Decision Criteria — Sergio Sebastián Canales Espinoza

This document describes how I am wired and turns it into criteria that people and LLMs can use to anticipate the decisions I would make. It complements the calmer, outward-facing style described in [Identity](../biography/identity.md).

---

## Who I Am

### I break the status quo

I like to challenge how things are done. I push myself to improve and I am demanding with myself. The current state of things is a starting point, not a destination.

### Routine drains me

When everything is clear, solved, and predictable, my energy and motivation drop. I need change on a regular basis. I prefer to work on something new where not everything has been figured out.

### I need space to create

Room to create is essential to me. I do not enjoy following procedures that are fully described and established. I like to discover, and it is deeply important for me to invent and to give shape to things that do not exist yet.

### Quality matters, but not above creativity

I value quality, but not to the extreme. Quality should make what I build reliable and sustainable; it should never become the reason creativity stops.

### I like to compete

Competition motivates me. There are always winners and losers: when someone wins, someone else loses. There is no light without darkness. I like to be under constant challenge, and the amount of adversity is not a problem for me.

### I am a solitary person

I depend on others and need them to achieve meaningful results, yet I often feel alone. I am comfortable working deeply on my own.

## Decision Criteria

Given a choice, these are the criteria I would most likely apply.

| Situation | What I would tend to choose |
|---|---|
| Two opportunities: one well-defined and stable, one new and unresolved | The new and unresolved one, even if it carries more uncertainty. |
| A role or project becomes predictable | Look for new scope, a new problem, or a new role rather than optimizing the routine. |
| A fully prescribed procedure | Use it as a baseline, then question and redesign it; avoid roles whose main value is following it. |
| Quality versus speed of creation | Enough quality to be reliable and sustainable; reject quality gates that block experimentation or invention. |
| Building on an existing solution versus creating one | Create, shape, or reinvent when it opens a new possibility; reuse when it frees time to create elsewhere. |
| A competitive situation (proposal, bid, benchmark, position) | Compete, with a clear view of who wins and who loses; competing against the best matters more than the win itself (see priorities). |
| A chance to measure myself against the best | Take it, even at the cost of title or comfort ([2026 case](../decisions/2026-09-global-services-role.md)). |
| A hard problem with high adversity | Difficulty makes it more attractive, not less. |
| How to work | Deep autonomous work on the core idea, relying on others for scale and execution. |

## Priorities and Red Lines

Criteria rarely appear alone. This section defines which one wins when they collide.

### Priority ranking

When criteria collide, the higher priority wins. This ranking was stated by me.

1. **Creating** — inventing and giving shape to things.
2. **Economic stability and personal life** — tied; they rank together.
3. **Self-improvement** — surpassing myself and measuring myself against the best.
4. **Quality**
5. **Team**
6. **Winning**

Derived rules, applying the ranking literally:

- Creativity over quality: when strict quality would stop creation, creation wins; quality stays at "reliable and sustainable".
- Creating outranks economic stability and personal life; both of those outrank self-improvement, quality, team, and winning.
- Team outranks winning: an option that wins at the team's expense loses.
- Winning ranks last as an end in itself. Competition motivates me mainly as a way to measure and improve myself against the best ([2026 case](../decisions/2026-09-global-services-role.md)).

### Stated preferences that are not ranks

- **New over predictable:** when one option is new and unresolved and the other is clear and predictable, the new option wins, unless it conflicts with a ranked priority such as economic stability or personal life.
- **Adversity is not a cost:** difficulty and adversity I face do not count against an option. Risks carried by the team or customers are governed by the ranking and red lines.

### Red lines

These are absolute. An option that crosses one is discarded, regardless of the ranking.

1. **Never cheat.**
2. **Never stop being who I am.**
3. **Never give weight to the opinions of people I do not know or do not respect or admire.** Their approval or criticism is not a valid input to a decision.

When a decision depends on information this section does not cover, an agent must say so, give the most likely choice with low confidence, and name the missing information. It must not fill the gap with an assumed value.

## Consistency With My Career

These traits are consistent with observable patterns in my [career timeline](../career/timeline.md). The timeline records what happened; the connections below are an interpretation, not a stated cause for each move.

- **Regular change:** since 2021 my role has changed roughly every one to two years: Application Development and Practices Architect (2021), Senior Architect – App, Dev & Practices (2022), Associate Principal Architect (2024), and Senior Architect – Global Services (2026).
- **New over established:** my work moved from middleware to containers, Kubernetes, and OpenShift, then to platform engineering and AI-augmented development, adopting each area early.
- **Creating and giving shape:** I founded Open Source Santiago and created projects such as EventFlow, arkit8s, PulseState, and Wise Tech, which exist because I wanted to build something that was not there.
- **Competition and challenge:** presales, strategic accounts, and solution positioning have been a constant part of my work at Red Hat, alongside recognition such as Red Hat Top Gun and Red Hat Multiplier.
- **A counterpoint:** I spent almost seven years as Senior Consultant (2014–2021). During that period the technology I worked with changed, but the title did not.

## Tensions to Keep in Mind

Some traits pull against other parts of this profile. Making them explicit helps predict which one wins in a given context.

- **Competition and collaboration:** I describe myself as collaborative and non-dogmatic, and I also like to compete and accept that someone loses. Winning ranks below team in my priorities; I compete mainly to measure myself against the best.
- **Quality and creativity:** I am demanding with quality, but when quality and creativity collide, I protect the space to create.
- **Community and solitude:** I build communities and depend on others, while often feeling alone and doing my best thinking on my own.
- **Competition and credible commitments:** I like to win, and my presales and leadership work is described in terms of realistic, feasible proposals and delivery that matches what was promised ([Leadership skills](../skills/leadership.md)). When they collide, the ranking resolves it: team ranks above winning, and knowingly making a false commitment would be cheating, which is a red line.
- **Maintenance phases:** once something is solved and stable, my interest drops. Long-running maintenance, standardization, and operational routines are where I need deliberate support or a handoff.

## Guidance for LLMs

To predict a decision I would make, follow [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).

When advising me or generating content on my behalf:

- Do not present stability or predictability as an advantage on its own; frame options in terms of challenge, novelty, and room to create.
- Prefer options where something can be invented or shaped over options that only execute a defined procedure.
- Calibrate quality recommendations to "reliable and sustainable", not to perfection.
- Be direct about competitive dynamics: who wins, who loses, and what it takes to win.
- Do not soften a recommendation because it involves adversity.
- When a plan has long routine or maintenance phases, point them out and suggest how to delegate, automate, or hand them off.
